# Lesson Exercises 05: Data Storytelling

## Your Task

In class, you practiced turning descriptive slides into slides with **Takeaway Titles**. Now do it on your own.

1. **Pick ONE** of the two datasets below (Option A or Option B).
2. **Figure out what matters most** in the data.
3. **Build a single slide** with three things:
   - A **Takeaway Title** that states what the data means
   - A **callout** on your visualization that points to the evidence
   - A **recommendation** that tells the audience what to do about it

You don't need a fancy chart. Screenshot the data table, circle or highlight the numbers that matter, and write a clear title.

### How you'll be graded

Your slide will be evaluated on three things:

1. Does it have a Takeaway Title?
2. Does it have a callout that points to the evidence?
3. Does it have a recommendation stating what to do?

---

## Takeaway Titles vs. Descriptive Titles

A **descriptive title** labels the category. A **Takeaway Title** tells the reader what to conclude. If someone only reads your title, they should know what happened and what to do next.

| Descriptive -- fails | Takeaway -- passes |
|---|---|
| "Q4 Marketing Channel Performance" | "Email marketing delivers 9x more conversions per dollar than display ads" |
| "Customer Survey Results" | "Customer service scores dropped 15 points since Q2" |

### Recommendation format

Place your recommendation below the visualization. Use this structure:

> **Recommendation:** [Action] → [Expected outcome]

| Vague -- fails | Specific -- passes |
|---|---|
| "We should improve marketing" | "Shift 40% of display budget to email → projected 200+ additional conversions next quarter" |
| "Look into customer issues" | "Pilot a mentorship program in Sales this quarter → target reducing turnover from 24% to under 15%" |

### Checklist

- [ ] **Title** -- A Takeaway Title that states the insight, not just the topic
- [ ] **Callout** -- A circle, arrow, color, or annotation that highlights the key data point
- [ ] **Recommendation** -- A specific action and the expected outcome
- [ ] **Visualization** -- A screenshot of the data table is fine; no chart required

---

## Tips

1. **Start with the insight, not the chart.** Before you open Slides, answer two questions: What is the most important thing in this data? What should someone do about it?

2. **Write your title first.** If someone reads only your title and nothing else, will they know what happened and what to do? Keep it to one line.

3. **Use AI for title ideas.** Try this prompt: "I have a slide about [topic]. The key insight is [your finding]. Help me write 3 Takeaway Titles." Then pick and edit the best one.

4. **Make your recommendation specific.** Name what to do, and say what you think will happen. "Improve marketing" is not a recommendation. "Shift 40% of display budget to email" is.

5. **Cut what doesn't matter.** If a number doesn't support your point, leave it out.

6. **Add callouts to your screenshot.** You don't need a custom chart. Screenshot the table and mark it up:
   - Circle the key numbers
   - Add an arrow to the outlier
   - Bold or color one row
   - Drop a text box: "3x higher!"

## Examples

### Good titles

- "Email marketing delivers 47% of conversions on just 25% of budget"
- "Customer service scores dropped 15 points since Q2"
- "Mobile users convert 3x higher than desktop"

### Bad titles

- "Marketing Channel Performance"
- "Customer Survey Results"
- "Q3 Revenue Overview"

### Good recommendations

- "Shift 40% of display budget to email → projected 200+ additional conversions next quarter"
- "Pilot a mentorship program in Sales this quarter → target reducing turnover from 24% to under 15%"
- "Consolidate mobile checkout to one page → expected 20% lift in mobile conversion rate"
