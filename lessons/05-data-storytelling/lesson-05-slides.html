Data Storytelling

Write titles that tell people what to do, not just what happened

5-Step Analytics Framework

  • 1 Define the business problem
  • 2 Collect and prepare the data
  • 3 Analyze the data and generate insights
  • 4 Communicate the insights, recommendations, and predictions
  • 5 Act and track the change

The Power of a Takeaway Title

Before: descriptive title
Q4 Marketing Channel Performance

Labels the topic. The CMO has to study the data and figure out the conclusion herself.

After: takeaway title
Email marketing delivers 9x more conversions per dollar than display ads

States what happened. The CMO reads the title and immediately knows where to reallocate budget.

Which slide helps the CMO decide faster?

What Makes a Takeaway Title

Your title is the most important part of the slide. It should answer: "So what?"

  • What is the main finding?
  • What should the audience do about it?
  • If they read nothing else, do they get it?

A descriptive title labels the category.
A Takeaway Title states the conclusion.

Same Data, Different Stories

Your title tells the audience how to feel about the data

Positive framing
NPS is showing growth over time
We're improving but still behind competitors
Net Promoter Score has room to grow
Negative framing
Our NPS needs urgent attention
Competitors are pulling ahead in NPS
We're in the bottom quartile of NPS

Same numbers, different stories. Pick the framing that matches your call to action.

Use Sentence Case

Takeaway Title
NPS is increasing over time ✓
Descriptive Title
NPS Over Time ✗

Sentence case signals a complete thought, not a label.

If it reads like a sentence, it's probably a Takeaway Title.

The Manager Test

A manager should be able to
just read the Takeaway Titles.

If they need supporting evidence,
they can look at the rest of the slide.

Quick test: If someone reads only your titles and skips everything else, do they get the findings and know what to do?

Adding Callouts to Your Data

Point the viewer's eye to what matters

You don't need fancy charts. Screenshot your data table and mark it up. See Chartr Newsletters for good examples.

What to add

  • Circle the most important numbers
  • Arrow pointing to the outlier
  • Bold or color one row
  • Text box with a note: "Lowest cost!"

How to add them

  • Slides / PowerPoint: Insert → Shape
  • Text box: Insert → Text box
  • Highlight: Select cell → Fill color
  • Paste the table as an image first

Write a Specific Recommendation

Place it below your visualization

Recommendation: [Action] → [Expected outcome]
Vague — fails

"We should improve marketing"

"Look into customer issues"

Specific — passes

"Shift 40% of display budget to email → projected 200+ additional conversions next quarter"

"Pilot a mentorship program in Sales → target reducing turnover from 24% to under 15%"
