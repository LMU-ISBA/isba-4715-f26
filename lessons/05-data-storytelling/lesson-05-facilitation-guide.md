# Lesson 05: Data Storytelling — Facilitation Guide

## Overview

- **Duration**: 90 minutes
- **Format**: In-person, group exercises with presentations
- **Core Skill**: Writing Takeaway Titles (insight-driven slide titles)
- **Framework**: Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic's *Storytelling with Data*

## Materials

- [Google Slides for Group Exercise #1](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/16HeLjU7ou7_x8jy4sElKXcO_3IhkKPsR-AxqmHbwGm4/edit?usp=sharing)
- Marketing Channel ROI dataset (below)
- Timer

## The Dataset

A startup allocated $100,000 equally across four marketing channels ($25,000 each):

| Channel | Spend | Impressions | Clicks | Conversions | Conv. Rate | Cost per Conv. |
|---------|-------|-------------|--------|-------------|------------|----------------|
| Paid Search | $25,000 | 500,000 | 15,000 | 350 | 2.3% | $71 |
| Social Media | $25,000 | 1,200,000 | 8,000 | 85 | 1.1% | $294 |
| Email Marketing | $25,000 | 150,000 | 12,000 | 420 | 3.3% | $60 |
| Display Ads | $25,000 | 2,000,000 | 4,000 | 45 | 1.1% | $556 |

**The insight students should find**: Email Marketing delivers 47% of all conversions with only 25% of budget, at the lowest cost per conversion ($60). Display Ads burn 25% of budget for only 5% of conversions at $556 each.

**The recommendation**: Reallocate budget from Display and Social to Email Marketing.

## Session Flow

### Block 1: Opening + DC ACT Framework (10 min)

Connect this lesson to the DC ACT framework students have been using all semester:

1. **D**efine the business problem
2. **C**ollect and prepare the data
3. **A**nalyze the data and generate insights
4. **C**ommunicate the insights ← **Today's focus**
5. **A**ct and track the change

You've spent Lessons 01–04 on steps 1–3. Today is about step 4: once you have the insight, how do you get someone to act on it?

The difference between a good analyst and a great one is communication. You can write the best SQL query in the world, but if your slide says "Marketing Channel Performance" and the CMO skims past it, nothing changes.

### Block 2: Scenario Introduction (8 min)

Present the marketing channel dataset. Walk through the data without revealing the insight. Let students see the numbers and start forming their own conclusions.

**Frame it**: "You're a data analyst at this startup. The CMO needs to decide how to allocate next quarter's $100,000 marketing budget. She has 30 seconds to look at your slide. What does she need to see?"

### Block 3: Group Exercise #1 — First Attempt (20 min)

**Instructions to students**:
- Get into groups of 3–4
- Open the shared Google Slides link
- Find your group's slide
- Create ONE slide: title, visualization (chart, table, or screenshot), something presentable in under 2 minutes
- You have 20 minutes

**What you do during this time**: Rotate through groups (~3–4 minutes each). Observe without correcting. Watch for:
- Title styles — descriptive ("Marketing Performance") vs. insight-driven
- Whether they highlight anything or just dump the full table
- Whether they include a recommendation or just show data
- Note which groups to select for presentations later

**Do NOT teach yet.** The whole point is that they produce something imperfect so the gap reveal lands harder.

Common mistakes you'll see (don't correct them):
- Descriptive titles: "Marketing Channel Performance Q4"
- Data dumps with no visual hierarchy
- No clear takeaway
- No recommendation

### Block 4: Gap Reveal + Knaflic Principles (12 min)

This is the most important moment in the lesson.

**Show a before/after comparison**:
- **Before**: A slide titled "Marketing Channel Performance" with a plain data table
- **After**: A slide titled "Email marketing delivers 9x more conversions per dollar than display ads" with the Email row highlighted and a recommendation line

Ask: "Which one helps the CMO decide faster?"

Then teach the principles:

**Takeaway Titles**:
- A descriptive title labels the category. A Takeaway Title states the conclusion.
- If someone only reads your title, they should know what happened and what to do.
- Use sentence case (signals a complete thought, not a label).
- Keep it to one line.

**The three elements of a good data slide**:
1. **Takeaway Title** — states the insight
2. **Callout** — highlights the evidence (circle, arrow, color, annotation)
3. **Recommendation** — tells the audience what to do: [Action] → [Expected outcome]

**Title examples**:

| Descriptive (fails) | Takeaway (passes) |
|---------------------|-------------------|
| "Q4 Marketing Channel Performance" | "Email marketing delivers 9x more conversions per dollar than display ads" |
| "Customer Survey Results" | "Customer service scores dropped 15 points since Q2" |
| "Website Traffic Analysis" | "Organic search drives 59% of conversions — double down on SEO" |

**Recommendation examples**:

| Vague (fails) | Specific (passes) |
|---------------|-------------------|
| "We should improve marketing" | "Shift 40% of display budget to email → projected 200+ additional conversions next quarter" |
| "Look into customer issues" | "Pilot a mentorship program in Sales → target reducing turnover from 24% to under 15%" |

### Block 5: Group Exercise #2 — Revision (10 min)

**Instructions to students**:
- Revise your slide using what you just learned
- Focus on your title first
- You can use AI to help brainstorm titles (encourage it)
- You have 10 minutes

**What you do**: Quick visits (~2 minutes per group). Now you actively coach.

**Coaching questions**:
- "What's the one thing the CMO should remember from your slide?"
- "If she only reads your title, will she know what to do?"
- "Where's your recommendation?"

**During this time, select 3 groups for presentations**:
1. **Strong example** — clear Takeaway Title, good callout, solid recommendation
2. **Needs coaching** — descriptive title or missing recommendation (opportunity to improve live)
3. **Interesting approach** — creative or unexpected angle

### Block 6: Group Presentations (20 min)

3 groups present, ~7 minutes each (3 min present + 4 min feedback).

**Presentation format**: Group walks through their slide — what's the problem, what did the data show, what should the CMO do?

**Feedback questions to ask**:
- "What's the title telling us?"
- "If you saw this slide tomorrow, what would you remember?"
- "Is the recommendation specific enough? Would the CMO know exactly what to do?"
- "What's one thing that could make this slide stronger?"

For the group that needs coaching, help them rewrite the title live. Ask the class: "How could we turn this descriptive title into a Takeaway Title?" This is a teaching moment, not a gotcha.

### Block 7: Wrap-Up + Lesson Exercises (10 min)

Summarize with three things to remember:
1. Your title is your headline — make it state the conclusion, not the category
2. Point to the evidence — circle it, arrow it, highlight it
3. Tell them what to do — a specific action with a predicted outcome

Introduce the take-home lesson exercises:
- Choose Option A (Website Traffic) or Option B (Employee Satisfaction)
- Build one slide with Takeaway Title + callout + recommendation
- Submit as a single-page PDF
- Graded on three things: Does it have a Takeaway Title? A callout? A recommendation?

---

## If You're Running Long

- Cut Exercise #1 to 15 min (visit fewer groups)
- Cut Gap Reveal to 10 min (fewer examples)
- Present 2 groups instead of 3

## If You're Running Short

- Have additional groups share just their titles (no full presentation)
- Deeper discussion on one example
- Let students start the take-home in class
